Mortgage Fraud and Covert Operations
The narrative of financial malfeasance often begins with the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). During her tenure as Assistant Secretary of Housing and Federal Housing Commissioner in the first Bush Administration (1989-1990), Catherine Austin Fitts was tasked with the formidable challenge of restoring HUD's image in the wake of the savings and loan crisis. A critical observation made by Fitts upon assuming office was the department's immense $300 billion mortgage insurance portfolio, managed by a strikingly inadequate single certified actuary. This lack of oversight capacity, it is argued, created fertile ground for financial irregularities.
After her departure from government service, Fitts established Hamilton Securities, an employee-owned brokerage house. In 1993, Hamilton Securities secured a contract with HUD to manage its substantial $500 billion investment portfolio. During this period, Fitts developed a location-based mortgage debt resale software program known as "Community Wizard," which reportedly led to an increase in department revenue by hundreds of millions of dollars. However, in 1997, HUD abruptly canceled the Hamilton contract, citing alleged accounting errors. This cancellation triggered an investigation into Hamilton Securities by the HUD Inspector-General, the FBI, and the Securities and Exchange Commission. Fitts contends that this investigation was not a legitimate inquiry but rather an act of retribution against her, asserting that the Community Wizard data had exposed the fraudulent issuance of some federally-guaranteed mortgage securities. Despite the allegations and the ensuing investigation, no evidence of wrongdoing by Fitts or Hamilton Securities was found when the case was closed in 2002.
A central claim in Fitts's analysis is that HUD's ostensible mission of fostering economic growth is, in reality, secondary to its function as a funding mechanism for military and intelligence agencies. She alleges that HUD systematically overpays for public housing rehabilitation, and the resulting difference is then funneled into unaudited "black budget" programs at the behest of national security agencies. This alleged system of financial diversion is given legal cover by the 1949 CIA Act, which authorized government agencies to transfer funds to or from the CIA "without regard to any provisions of law limiting or prohibiting transfers between appropriations". This legislative provision, it is argued, effectively created a "black budget" that allows billions to be spent annually by "unknown entities," serving to "confuse the public and belittle the Constitution".
The analysis suggests a direct and systemic connection between apparent inefficiencies and fraud within HUD's mortgage portfolio and the funding of undisclosed covert operations. The process begins with HUD's massive mortgage insurance portfolio, which, as observed by Fitts, was managed with insufficient oversight. The "Community Wizard" software, intended for transparency, allegedly exposed the fraudulent issuance of federally-guaranteed mortgage securities. These fraudulent activities, coupled with alleged overpayments for public housing rehabilitation, are depicted as creating excess funds. These funds are then purportedly diverted into unaudited "black budget programs" for military and intelligence agencies. The CIA Act of 1949 provides the legal framework for such transfers, allowing these funds to be spent outside constitutional accountability. The mission of The Solari Report is explicitly stated as helping individuals "build wealth in ways that build real wealth in the wider economy," with a focus on both "family wealth" and "community wealth". This approach directly counters the extractive nature of the "economic warfare" paradigm.
Fitts strongly advises against engaging with large banks that are implicated in financial frauds, instead recommending seeking out "great local community bank or credit union" that can be trusted. This emphasis on local financial institutions is a practical step towards decentralizing financial power.
To further empower communities, Fitts developed the "Solari" concept. A "Solari" is envisioned as an investment advisor and databank specifically designed for local neighborhoods (defined as 10,000 people or less). Its purpose is to promote transparency and facilitate the reengineering of capital within that specific locality. The ultimate goal of this initiative is to return economic control to the people at the local level. Fitts's overall work is dedicated to ethical investing, advocating for a sustainable economic future through equitable and ethical financial practices.
The analysis suggests that Fitts's proposed solutions directly counter the centralization and "economic warfare" she describes. By emphasizing "family wealth" and "community wealth" and advocating for local financial institutions, she promotes a bottom-up approach to economic empowerment. The "Solari" concept is presented as a practical framework for achieving this local control and transparency, enabling communities to manage their own money flow and resist external financial manipulation. This strategy aims to build resilient local economies that are less susceptible to the "harvesting" mechanisms of the centralized system.
Protecting Financial Transaction Freedom
A critical strategy for preserving individual and community autonomy involves actively resisting the imposition of digital financial control. Fitts strongly advocates for the continued use of physical cash and checks, and encourages local businesses and banks to support these traditional payment systems. This is presented as a direct countermeasure against the encroaching "all digital transaction system" that could enable unprecedented surveillance and control.
Central to this resistance is the concept of financial sovereignty for individuals and states. To further this independence from centralized financial systems, Fitts suggests that state lawmakers explore establishing state depositories for real assets, such as gold, silver, and digital records. This measure would provide an alternative to relying solely on federal or international financial infrastructures.
The analysis suggests that given the perceived threat of CBDCs and digital IDs enabling total control over financial transactions and personal behavior, Fitts's strategy for "financial transaction freedom" is presented as a direct act of resistance. By advocating for the continued use of cash and checks and promoting state-level financial sovereignty, she provides actionable steps for individuals and local entities to opt out of or reduce their reliance on the centralized digital control system. This approach is about creating parallel systems that allow for greater individual autonomy and freedom from surveillance and potential financial cutoff.
Reclaiming Governance
Beyond individual financial strategies, a broader imperative exists to reclaim and reform governance structures. A core tenet of Fitts's work is the demand for radical transparency and accountability in government spending, particularly concerning the alleged "black budget" and the trillions of dollars that remain unaccounted for. This transparency is deemed essential for an informed citizenry to hold its representatives accountable.
The analysis calls for a fundamental reform of the "central banking-warfare model" itself. This implies dismantling the systemic integration of financial institutions and military-intelligence operations that purportedly operate outside constitutional oversight. A key aspect of this reform involves ensuring that information and clearance systems are owned, controlled, and operated by government employees, rather than by private defense contractors.
Furthermore, Fitts emphasizes the critical importance of strengthening local governments and promoting decentralization. This approach seeks to shift responsibility and power away from centralized federal entities and towards local communities, enabling greater self-governance and responsiveness to citizen needs.
The analysis suggests that these measures aim to restore constitutional governance and public oversight by dismantling the mechanisms of the "deep state" and empowering local communities. This involves not only demanding transparency in financial matters but also fundamentally restructuring the relationship between government, central banks, and private contractors to ensure that power resides with the people and their elected representatives.
Moral and Spiritual Grounding
Underpinning all of Catherine Austin Fitts's proposed solutions is a profound emphasis on moral and spiritual grounding. She frequently frames the current global predicament as a "spiritual battle," urging individuals to make a conscious choice between aligning with "God or the machine". This perspective elevates the struggle beyond mere economic or political terms, positioning it as a fundamental conflict over human values and autonomy.
Fitts consistently stresses the paramount importance of personal integrity and trust in all business dealings. She encourages individuals to prioritize the quality and integrity of the people they choose to do business with, highlighting this as a crucial defense against the pervasive "scams, padded bills and problems in the quality of service" that arise from dealing with large, fraudulent institutions. This focus on ethical conduct at the individual level is seen as a powerful force for positive change.
Ultimately, Fitts's approach highlights the transformative power of individual intention and action. By fostering personal integrity, prioritizing ethical relationships, and consciously choosing to support transparent and community-oriented financial systems, individuals can collectively contribute to a more equitable and free future. This perspective underscores that the ability to stop the "downward spiral" is not solely dependent on large-scale political or economic reforms but also on a fundamental shift in individual consciousness and moral commitment.
The analysis suggests that Fitts frames the struggle as a moral and spiritual one, where individual integrity and a commitment to ethical principles are foundational to resisting the control grid and reclaiming a humane future. This perspective provides a guiding philosophy for the practical strategies she advocates, emphasizing that true freedom and wealth are built on a foundation of trust, transparency, and adherence to higher principles.
Conclusions
The comprehensive analysis of Catherine Austin Fitts's perspectives reveals a meticulously constructed narrative of systemic financial fraud and control, operating beneath the veneer of conventional governance. The core argument posits an ongoing "economic warfare" designed to centralize wealth and power, effectively "harvesting" the populace through mechanisms embedded within the financial system.
The alleged diversion of trillions of dollars in "unauthorized spending" from the U.S. Department of Defense and Housing and Urban Development into secret, unaudited channels stands as a central pillar of this narrative. This immense unaccounted sum is presented not as an error but as evidence of a parallel financial system, potentially funding a "breakaway civilization" of elites and their clandestine projects, including secret underground bases and space programs. This suggests a hidden agenda that transcends public oversight and democratic accountability.
The critique of the central banking model highlights its inherent design flaws, particularly the debt-based creation of money, as a mechanism for wealth debasement and centralized control. Institutions like the Bank for International Settlements are implicated in facilitating opaque financial flows, enabling central bankers to assert undue influence over fiscal policy and individual transactions. This financial centralization is further exacerbated by the deep integration of private technology and defense contractors into core government functions, leading to a technocratic-financial takeover of governance.
The emergence of Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs) is viewed as the ultimate tool for this "control grid," threatening to usher in an era of "modern slavery" by enabling comprehensive surveillance and the ability to control individual behavior through financial means. This technological evolution extends to concerns about the "Internet of Bodies," where human biology and consciousness could become integrated into a monitored "technology platform," further eroding autonomy.
The societal implications of this alleged system are profound, including a purported intentional policy of introducing drugs into communities that contributes to declining life expectancy. The historical context provided by Joseph Farrell's work suggests that this "breakaway civilization" may have roots in post-World War II developments, linking it to a long-standing agenda of technological and financial control. The alleged suppression of advanced energy sources and the genetic reengineering of the food supply are seen as further mechanisms to ensure central control over life's essentials.
To counter this "downward spiral," Fitts advocates for a multi-faceted approach centered on empowering individuals and local communities. This includes a strong emphasis on ethical investing, redirecting wealth towards community-building initiatives, and fostering financial resilience through local banks and the "Solari" concept. Crucially, active resistance to digital financial control through the continued use of cash and the promotion of state-level financial sovereignty is encouraged. Ultimately, the path to reclaiming freedom and wealth is framed as a moral and spiritual battle, demanding integrity, transparency, and a conscious choice to prioritize human well-being over the mechanisms of control.
